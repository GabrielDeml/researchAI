### topic-does-a-larger-evaluation-budget-reveal-a-s — Topic **Does a larger evaluation budget reveal a success-rate difference?** Repeat the paired experiment at 100,000, 250,000, and 500,000 evaluations while retaining shared initialization and operator-schedule controls. These should be treated as distinct, prospectively specified hypotheses rather than retrospective extensions of the present result. _(auto-enqueued follow-up from genetic-algorithms)_

Refuted: increasing the budget to 100,000, 250,000, or 500,000 evaluations revealed no one-block serial-update advantage over frozen-parent batch-16 runs; the paired mean difference was −0.078125 solved blocks at every checkpoint, with a 95% bootstrap upper bound of 0.359375—below the hypothesized one block. Caveat: neither algorithm reached the optimum in any of 64 pairs, so the experiment could not resolve small or rare success-rate differences.

### topic-can-the-optimization-comparison-be-repeate — Topic **Can the optimization comparison be repeated in a preregistered, informative regime?** Select the population size, mutation rate, and evaluation budget in advance using a separate calibration procedure so that serial success is measurable but nonsaturated. Then preregister the primary paired success effect, confidence interval, sample size, and decision rule before comparing batch-size-16. _(auto-enqueued follow-up from genetic-algorithms)_

The hypothesized large calibration gap was refuted: the median validation-minus-calibration target deviation was \(D=0.0234\), with a 95% bootstrap interval of \([0.0078, 0.0391]\), well below the prespecified 0.08 threshold. This indicates modest calibration optimism, not the predicted large effect. The analysis is exploratory rather than confirmed preregistered because it used 120 instead of the specified 40 splits without a supplied time-stamped protocol; serial replacement also outperformed frozen-parent batch-16 under serial-selected settings.

Report: `projects/topic-can-the-optimization-comparison-be-repeate/report.md`

### topic-can-the-original-executable-source-code-ex — Topic Can the original executable source code, exact dependency versions, environment specification, and timestamped machine-readable outputs be archived under a persistent identifier? _(auto-enqueued follow-up from genetic-algorithms-2)_

Supported: raw ZIP hashes differed for all 50 equivalent bundle pairs, while canonicalization produced identical identifiers for all 50 after normalizing JSON, paths, permissions, and timestamps. This demonstrates deterministic identification for the prototype’s restricted normalization profile, not general RO-Crate or semantic equivalence. Caveat: the synthetic, single-implementation experiment lacked external artifacts, independent verification, negative controls, and cross-platform testing, so reproducibility and interoperability remain unproven.
