### genetic-algorithms-2 — Genetic algorithms

Refuted: the \((\mu+1)\) GA’s pooled offspring-attempt/cache-miss ratio was 1.294—not the hypothesized \(\ge 1.8\)—and its median advantage under attempted-candidate accounting remained greater than half its cache-miss advantage; only two of four required conditions held. This conclusion applies only to the tested implementations, configuration, 20,000-evaluation budget, and seeds 0–29, with independent auditing limited because code and raw per-seed data were unavailable.

Report: `projects/genetic-algorithms-2/report.md`

### genetic-algorithms — Genetic algorithms

The hypothesis was refuted: batch-16 replacement cut median end-to-end runtime by 87.0% (0.524 to 0.0680 seconds), but neither method reached the optimum in any of 30 seeds, so the predicted ≥15-percentage-point success loss was not observed. However, success preservation remains inconclusive because the 50,000-evaluation budget produced a completely floored outcome, and the runtime comparison also combined vectorization, selection frequency, array-processing costs, and snapshot-based parent selection.

Report: `projects/genetic-algorithms/report.md`

### when-does-vectorized-numpy-actually-lose-to-plai — When does vectorized NumPy actually lose to plain Python loops? Characterize the array-size crossover point for common elementwise operations on Apple Silicon, and test whether the crossover is operation-dependent.

The hypothesis was refuted: `np.where` first practically beat the Python loop at \(n=4\) on an Apple M3 Pro, with a 1.107× median speedup for the all-true mask, and the reported crossover bin was the same for the deterministic 50% mask. However, the stable-crossover conclusion cannot be verified because key random-mask and intermediate-size timings were not supplied; \(n=3\) was also untested, so the exact integer crossover remains uncertain.

Report: `projects/when-does-vectorized-numpy-actually-lose-to-plai/report.md`
