## Summary
A preregistered synthetic classification experiment will compare permutation-invariant lexicographic tie-breaking with first-encountered-maximum tie-breaking across 60 untouched replication datasets, 25 fixed grid permutations, and 120 fixed evaluation splits.

## Metrics
- Total-order invariant datasets: count out of 60 having exactly one selected configuration across 25 permutations and final-estimate range <=1e-12; the required threshold is 60.
- First-rule configuration-varying datasets: count out of 60 having more than one selected configuration across 25 permutations; the required threshold is at least 40.
- Reported-winner-change datasets: count out of 60 for which at least one first-encountered permutation selects the opposite optimizer label from the total-order selection; the required threshold is at least 15.
- Material-estimate-change datasets: count out of 60 with at least one first-rule final estimate differing from the total-order estimate by more than 0.005 balanced-accuracy units.
- Permutation-level selection-change rate: number of the 1500 dataset-permutation cases whose first-rule selected ID differs from the corresponding total-order ID, divided by 1500.
- Within-dataset estimate instability: maximum minus minimum first-rule 120-split estimates over the 25 permutations, summarized by median, interquartile range, and maximum.
- Bootstrap uncertainty: 95% dataset-level percentile intervals from 10000 resamples for the proportions with configuration variation, winner change, and material estimate change.

## Expected outcomes
- **If supported:** All 60 datasets will have exactly one total-order configuration and a final-estimate range <=1e-12 across permutations; at least 40 will have multiple first-rule selections; and at least 15 will switch the reported optimizer winner relative to total order. The heatmap will show permutation-dependent configuration and optimizer colors under first encounter while total-order results remain invariant.
- **If refuted:** The joint hypothesis is refuted if even one dataset fails total-order invariance, fewer than 40 datasets have multiple first-rule selections, or fewer than 15 datasets change the reported optimizer winner; a largely uniform first-rule heatmap and near-zero estimate ranges would indicate that encounter order had little practical effect.
