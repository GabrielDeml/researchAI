# Literature survey: Sampling distributions and pass probabilities across independently selected master seeds

Preregistration is central to interpreting repeated experiments because it fixes the analysis and decision rules before outcomes are observed. Lei, Gelman, and Ghitza distinguish procedural reproduction from preregistered replication with new data, emphasizing that preregistration limits researcher degrees of freedom and reduces the risk of mistaking noise for stable structure [1]. Sarfati and Vilfort make the frequentist rationale explicit: when analysis rules are fixed ex ante, nominal properties such as test size and interval coverage are defined over hypothetical identical repetitions of the experiment [2]. They also show why post hoc changes are problematic: if reporting or specification choices depend on realized outcomes, the resulting estimators and confidence intervals are conditioned on selection and may no longer have their conventional interpretation [2]. For a seed-sensitive computational experiment, the analogous requirement is that all five pass/fail conditions, seed-generation rules, exclusions, aggregation procedures, and failure handling remain fixed across master seeds.

The retrieved reproducibility literature establishes that random-seed variation is an empirical property worth measuring, but provides little direct evidence about full sampling distributions. McDermott et al. identify inadequate code and data accessibility as obstacles to reproducibility in machine learning, supporting the need to preserve executable code, configurations, and generated outputs for every repetition [3]. Merilehto provides the closest concrete example of replication across random seeds: three independent runs produced standard deviations below 0.5% for the reported malware-detection results [6]. This demonstrates one common summary—between-seed standard deviation—but three runs are insufficient for estimating tail behavior or precise pass probabilities. Recent preregistered computational evaluations also illustrate useful controls: Iscan fixes models and evaluates them under preregistered rules [4], while Cochran freezes byte-identical content across experimental arms and uses blind grading against verified references [5]. Such controls help ensure that variation attributed to master seeds is not confounded with changing models, corpora, access structures, or evaluation procedures.

Monte Carlo and sequential Monte Carlo work supplies the broad statistical framework for the proposed repetition study. Metropolis methods treat simulation outputs as draws generated by a stochastic algorithm and highlight that dependence and algorithmic structure matter for interpreting Monte Carlo variation [8]. Salomone et al. similarly stress that simulated samples need not be independent within an algorithm and derive convergence and unbiasedness properties without assuming such independence [9]. Eberle and Marinelli analyze probability-measure flows and asymptotic stability for sequential Monte Carlo samplers, including settings where ordinary MCMC mixes slowly [10]. Applied here, each independently generated master seed should define one complete experimental replicate; the replicate—not an internal generation, arm, or trial—is therefore the natural sampling unit. Across replicates, the primary outputs should include each condition’s empirical sampling distribution and marginal pass probability, along with the joint distribution of the five pass indicators. Reporting only an overall “all conditions pass” rate would conceal which conditions dominate failure and whether failures co-occur.

## Gap

None of the retrieved sources estimates the sampling distributions and pass probabilities of five preregistered conditions when an entire computational experiment is repeated over many independently selected master seeds. The literature supports preregistered decision rules, frozen experimental components, independent top-level repetitions, and explicit characterization of stochastic variability [1][2][4][5][6], but it does not determine how stable the present five conditions are. The new experiment should therefore estimate, without post hoc threshold changes, each condition’s pass probability, the uncertainty and distribution of its underlying statistic, dependence among condition outcomes, and the probability that all five conditions pass simultaneously. The retrieved material is too thin to predict those quantities or justify treating a single master seed as representative.
